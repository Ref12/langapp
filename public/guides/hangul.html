Guide

Learn to read and write 한글 (Hangul)

Korean has its own alphabet, small enough to learn in a day or two. This page is a reference: how the letters fit together, what each one sounds like, how to write it, and the few sound changes you meet early. The lessons start with words and do not teach the letters, so use this page beside them.

1

How Hangul works

40letters in all (21 vowels, 19 consonants)
14 + 10basic consonants and vowels: learn these first
5 + 11doubled consonants and combined vowels, built from the basic ones

Letters are not written in a line. They are stacked into square syllable blocks, one block for each syllable. A block is an initial consonant + a vowel (+ an optional final consonant, the 받침). If a syllable starts with a vowel sound, the silent consonant ㅇ holds the first place, so every block has a consonant on top or on the left.

나 na = ㄴ+ㅏ

그 geu = ㄱ+ㅡ
Mixed vowelㄱㅗㅏThe vowels ㅘ ㅙ ㅚ ㅝ ㅞ ㅟ ㅢ have a horizontal and a vertical part: the consonant sits at the top left and the vowel wraps round it (ㅘ = ㅗ under the consonant, ㅏ beside it).과 gwa = ㄱ+ㅘ
위 wi = ㅇ+ㅟ
With a finalㅎㅏㄴThe final consonant goes in the bottom row, under the vowel, and is read last.한 han = ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄴ
Final under a horizontal vowelㄱㅡㄹThree rows: consonant, vowel, final.글 geul = ㄱ+ㅡ+ㄹ

So 한글 Hangeul is two blocks: 한 (ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄴ) and 글 (ㄱ+ㅡ+ㄹ). Read blocks left to right, and inside a block read the consonant, then the vowel, then the final. The name means "Korean script" (한 Korean + 글 writing); it is often spelled "Hangul".

A final consonant is written with the same letters as an initial one, but only seven sounds are possible at the end of a syllable. They are listed in section 2.

The letters

Names and order are the official ones (NIKL). Sounds are for standard (Seoul) Korean. "Romanization" is the Revised Romanization used on signs and in the app. Examples are common words with their romanization and meaning.

Writing

Stroke order rules
1Top to bottom, left to right.
2Inside a block: initial consonant, then vowel, then final consonant.
3In a vowel, the stroke that is further up or further left comes first: ㅏ the stem, then the tick on its right; ㅓ the tick on the left, then the stem; ㅗ the short upright, then the bar under it; ㅜ the bar, then the short stem under it.
4In ㅁ the left side comes first, then the top and right side in one stroke, then the bottom; in ㅂ the two uprights, then the middle bar, then the bottom bar. The circle ㅇ is one stroke, from the top, anticlockwise. A short tick on top (ㅊ ㅎ) comes first.
5A doubled consonant is the single letter twice, left one first (ㄲ = ㄱ + ㄱ).

Sounds change between syllables

Korean is not said letter by letter. Where two syllables meet, the sounds adjust, and the spelling does not follow. The rules are regular. Five come up in the first lessons. "Said" shows the standard pronunciation written in Hangul, to show the change.

Linking

A final consonant moves onto a following syllable that starts with the silent ㅇ.

Palatalisation

A final ㄷ or ㅌ before the suffix 이 (or 히) is said j or ch. This only happens at the join of a word and its ending, not in every word: 티켓 stays "tiket".

Palatalisation
WrittenRomanizationSaidNoteWhere

굳이guji구지insistentlyNIKL example
닫히다dachida다치다to be closedNIKL example

Why the app's readings follow the pronunciation. The official Revised Romanization is a transcription of how Korean is said (rule 1 of the NIKL system), not a letter-for-letter transliteration. A reading that spells 입니다 as "ibnida" teaches a sound nobody says, so the app's readings follow the sound: imnida. The official system leaves out tensing (so 학생 is haksaeng, said "hak-ssaeng"), and a hyphen or doubled letter in brackets on this page only helps you hear it.
