# Korean as its own app: inventory and staged plan

Written 2026-11-10 on public main at `2c6fd07` (branch `langapp/korean-plan`). Read-only: no source file was changed and no test suite was run. Looked at with `npm ci`, a complete Japanese build (`LANGAPP_LANGUAGE=ja npm run build:next`: the four checks, `tsc -b` and `vite build`, all passed) and a throwaway Korean build (`LANGAPP_LANGUAGE=ko` `vite build` into the scratch folder, served on a local port, opened in headless Chrome at phone width). Nothing from either build is committed. No screenshots are committed: the Korean app has no lesson text yet, so one Overview shot would add nothing (fonts-noto-cjk was installed; the page text read back as real Hangul).

Read first: `2026-11-10-step-4-plan.md` (how the old parallel screens went), `2026-11-10-ja-practice-plan.md` (stages A to E and word forms), `2026-11-10-ja-content-gaps.md`, `2026-11-10-ja-forms.md`, `2026-11-10-ja-dictionary-look.md`, `2026-11-10-ja-writing-on.md`.

## 0. In one page

- Korean already has more behind the seam than the step-4 plan said. The pack **stubs exist**: `koMeta` (meta.ts), `koIdentity`, `koSentence`, `koPage`, `koWordForms`, `koPractice`, `koDictionary`, `koWriting`, a Korean particle table, the forms engine (`core/forms/korean.mjs`), the Korean dictionary reader, a Korean service worker name (`sw-lab-ko.js`) and the build constants. What is missing is the **data pack** (`src/data/packs/ko`, nothing today: `koPack` is `emptyData`), its generator, the published flag and the workflow job, the Korean reading display, and Korean tests.
- The lesson source is good enough to start: 87 of 87 Chinese lessons have Korean words, sentences and a Chinese line mapping (300 words, 801 sentences, 107 grammar points). Its faults are of the kind the Japanese gap work already found and fixed (unused words, grammar never shown in a sentence, grammar-less lessons), plus Korean-only ones: no readings anywhere, a syllable-by-syllable romanizer that gets common words wrong, mixed Korean/English part-of-speech labels, and a dictionary whose English "glosses" are long dictionary definitions.
- A Korean build **already compiles and runs** today (empty): Overview, Lessons, Practice and Settings draw; Dictionary and Writing say "Not available in the Korean app yet." Nothing crashes, the console is clean. It cannot be published because `published` is false and there is no workflow job.
- Plan: 9 small stages. The first publishes `lab/ko/` (empty app, Hangul writing on) and needs no data. Then readings, the data pack, content corrections, Practice, word forms, Dictionary and Writing can mostly run side by side (section 3 marks them).

### 1.2 Readings (Hangul and romanization)

- Korean is written in Hangul, which is the text. The only "reading" a learner needs is a romanization (and, for some words, how they are pronounced: 학생 is said `학쌩`).
- **No file holds a reading.** In `src/data/dictionary/ko.json` the reading field is empty for all 11,069 words; the lessons' `reading` field is empty by design (the writer prompt says "Korean: leave empty").
- A romanizer exists: `romanizeKorean` in `src/core/language/romanize.ts`, which is **Revised Romanization letter by letter with no sound changes**. Tried on real words, it gives: 감사합니다 → `gamsahapnida` (RR: gamsahamnida), 좋아요 → `johayo` (joayo), 같이 → `gati` (gachi), 십육 → `sipyuk` (simnyuk), 입니다 → `ipnida` (imnida), 값 → `gaps` (gap), 읽어요 → `ilkeoyo` (ilgeoyo). It is right for simple words (학교 hakgyo, 한국어 hangugeo). Because a learner hears and says the changed sound, this is wrong in exactly the words of the first lessons.
- **An official pronunciation list exists**: `curriculum/korean/reading-overlay.yaml` (NIKL citation pronunciations, 11,590 entries, 11,074 with a pronunciation). For the 259 NIKL words of the lessons: 252 have one, and **101 differ from the spelling** (학생 → 학쌩, 중국어 → 중구거, 좋다 → 조ː타, 춥다 → 춥따), with length marks (ː) that the app should not show. It covers no inflected forms, so sentences and conjugated forms still need a rule-based pronunciation function.
- Hanja: only 1 lesson word carries a hanja field. Nothing to use.
- Romanization scheme to offer: Revised Romanization (the one the dictionary and the guides use). Not McCune-Reischauer. Yale for no one.

### 1.3 Word forms

`curriculum/korean/forms.yaml` (118 lines; built into `src/data/forms/ko.json`, read by `core/forms/korean.mjs`): **12 stem classes** (regular vowel and consonant stems, ㄹ, ㅡ, 하다, and the ㅂ, ㄷ, ㅅ, 르, ㅎ irregulars, the copulas 이다 and 아니다), lemma lists for the irregulars, and **30 forms**: dictionary, 해요체 present, past, future, wish, honorific, -지 않아요, 안 + verb, -(으)ㄹ까요, 주세요, connectors -고 and -아서/어서, purpose, two modifiers, can/cannot, know-how, progressive, prohibit, permit, must, resultive, promise, intend, nominalised -기, quote, past negative, and **formal polite `-ㅂ니다/습니다`**. Each form names the grammar point that teaches it (some are the course-written `ko-par-g062`, `g029`, `g030`).

Compared with Japanese: Japanese has two display forms (polite ます and dictionary), one reading and one writing system per word. Korean has **speech levels**: the learner needs the dictionary form (먹다) to look a word up, the everyday polite form (먹어요) for almost all sentences in the data, and the formal form (먹습니다) in the forms table only; it has no plain/casual or honorific-speech set beyond `honorific`. Conjugation is **stem-based with sound rules** (contraction 오+아→와, irregulars) rather than Japanese's ending tables, so the Korean engine is already a different engine behind the same `PackWordForms` interface, with `engineWordForms('ko')` wired and "no catalog yet, so nothing is ever asked". The old parallel data had 10 irregular items as lesson-scoped `ko:f:<lemma>:<form>` rows; those are not a pack feature yet. Not checked: that every lesson word classifies correctly (the Japanese stage did this per word in `ja-forms.md`).

### 2.2 Needs a Korean variant that Japanese did not have

1. A reading display whose "script" is a romanization of the same text (Japanese has a different script, kana, for kanji and romaji from kana).
2. A pronunciation (sound-change) function: Korean pronunciation is not the spelling; RR of the spelling is not what is heard.
3. Spacing as a unit of the sentence (tiles are words between spaces), and particles written attached to the noun (tokens split them).
4. Speech levels in forms and in the sentences the course writes.
5. A different dictionary: sense-heavy long definitions and 846 repeated headwords.
6. Hangul compose/decompose for a Hangul-aware search and for the writing viewer to show the jamo of a syllable.

## 3. Plan: nine stages

Same shape as the Japanese plan: small, each publishable at `lab/ko/`, the Chinese app and the Japanese app unchanged (no Chinese screen pin or Japanese pin moves, unless a stage says so and says why). ## 4. Questions for the owner

**1. Which way of speaking should the Korean lessons show by default?**
Korean has several levels of politeness. The lessons' 801 sentences are all in the everyday polite style (먹어요), the formal style (먹습니다) is only in the table of word forms, and the dictionary lists verbs as 먹다.
- A. Everyday polite for sentences, verbs shown as 먹다 in word lists, the learner can switch to formal or dictionary form in Settings (recommended: it is what the data is written in, and what a learner needs for speaking; this mirrors the Japanese default).
- B. Formal (먹습니다) everywhere: sounds like a textbook or the news, needs the 801 sentences rewritten.
- C. Show everyday polite and formal side by side in every lesson: most complete, more to read on a phone, and it would double the sentences in Practice.

**2. Show only Hangul, or Hangul with romanization underneath?**
The Chinese lessons begin with 저는 학생이에요 and the app does not teach the letters first.
- A. Hangul with romanization underneath by default, switchable to Hangul only in Settings (recommended for a beginner who cannot read Hangul yet; the romanization will follow how the word is pronounced, so 학생 shows haksaeng).
- B. Hangul only by default, romanization switchable on (for learners who learn the letters first).
- C. Only one of them, no setting (simplest, least friendly to a beginner or a returning learner).

**3. Should the app teach Hangul itself before the first lesson?**
The writing area will have all the letters (and syllables); the lessons themselves start with words.
- A. No: the letters live in the Writing area, with a note on the Lessons page pointing to them (recommended: lessons stay "the same meaning in every language", as decided).
- B. A short set of letter-and-sound lessons before lesson 1 (about 10 small lessons): more work, makes the Korean order differ from the other apps.
- C. A single chart page in the Writing area showing every letter with its sound: small work, still no new lessons.

**4. How far should the Korean course go after the 87 lessons?**
Japanese stops at the same 87 lessons (the first level of the Chinese course). Korean also has a large, separate collection arranged by the TOPIK exam levels (about 11,000 words in six levels), which is today used only as the dictionary.
- A. Stop at 87 lessons like Japanese, with the TOPIK words as the Dictionary only (recommended: the owner wants the same experience in every language; the plan is already nine stages).
- B. Add more lessons that follow the next Chinese levels, as the Chinese course grows.
- C. Build a separate TOPIK course from that collection, in its own order (a different course from the Chinese lessons; needs its own plan, since the order and meanings would no longer match).

**5. Is publishing the Korean dictionary under its share-alike licence acceptable?**
The word list comes from the National Institute of Korean Language's dictionary, which allows use (also commercial) if the app credits it, says what was changed, and lets anyone reuse the adapted word list under the same licence (Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike). The Korean app would show that credit, and the Korean word data would be open under that licence.
- A. Yes, publish with the credit and the licence notice (recommended; it is what the files already assume).
- B. Publish the lessons only and leave the big word list out until the owner has a lawyer's opinion (the app works without it; Dictionary stays off).
- C. Ask for a different, permissively licensed word list (none is in the repo; extra work, smaller list).

Technical choices made here, not asked: Revised Romanization with sound changes, pronunciations from the official list where it has one; one `koMeta` line per stage; Hangul writing guides stay AI-reviewed as decided on 2026-11-08; games, assistant and media off; Korean sentences never ask for a model; the Chinese app and the Japanese app change by no pin.
