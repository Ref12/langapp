# Stage 6 — nuanced professional and academic communication

**Version:** 2026-09-07. **Exam orientation:** TOPIK II, award level 6.
**Educational reference:** NIKL Standard Curriculum 2020-54 level 6,
paraphrased: professional/academic discourse, sociocultural or academic texts,
persuasion, recommendations and logically effective arguments.
This is not a claim that passing TOPIK establishes native-speaker equivalence,
complete domain expertise or mastery of every grammar construction.

## Prerequisites and diagnostic

Stage 5 outcomes: systematic reporting, synthesis, counterarguments, qualified
inference, coherent writing and audience-sensitive interaction. Diagnose using
two partially conflicting sources, a briefing and an evidence-based revision.
Locate any unresolved lower-stage weaknesses in quotation, voice, scope,
spacing, sound processes or register before emphasizing rarer constructions.

## Communicative outcomes

- **Listening:** track complex arguments, rhetorical concessions, implications,
  disagreement and uncertainty in unfamiliar professional/academic discussions.
- **Speaking:** persuade or recommend with an explicit evidential basis,
  respond precisely to challenges, reformulate for a non-specialist and repair
  an overstatement.
- **Reading:** analyze dense expository, formal and selected literary prose;
  distinguish literal claims, rhetorical questions, presupposition and stance.
- **Writing:** synthesize multiple perspectives in an extended report or
  argument; qualify causation, delimit scope and revise after criticism.
- **Pragmatics/phonology:** manage prominence, pauses, turn-taking and
  disagreement; recognize formal/literary endings without overusing them in
  normal conversation.

For `general / source uncategorized`, inspect English definitions, POS and
source record rather than relying on a topic label. Build a domain glossary
with sense-specific IDs and English meanings; reuse prior-stage words for
clarity. Some specialist or literary constructions are **recognition-first**:
productive mastery means selecting them appropriately, including choosing a
simpler alternative when that serves the audience better.

## Practice, review and transfer

Use a seminar response, an executive summary, a respectful policy disagreement,
an evidence audit, a plain-language rewrite of formal prose and a limited
literary interpretation. For any Korean source extract, generated prompt,
model or correction, provide an English counterpart in the tutor materials.
Use licensed or original texts; do not copy protected exam or course passages.

Ask the learner to mark claim, evidence, assumption, counterexample and
uncertainty. Revise a claim when the supplied evidence changes. Correct
unsupported causation and excessive generalization before ornamental style.
Sensitive social/cultural topics must be discussed without treating a nation,
age group or community as homogeneous.

Maintain ID-based retrieval at 1/3/7/14/30 days and portfolio reviews after
blocks 3, 6, 9 and 12. Include earlier grammar in unfamiliar contexts. Rehearse
prosody and turn-taking through a recorded briefing and reflective replay,
using live/tutor audio only with appropriate permission. Listening exposure
to different speakers should not imply a single regional accent is defective.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

| Skill | Task | Evidence for score 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | A 4–6-minute unfamiliar professional/academic discussion | Reconstruct positions, supporting evidence, implied stance and qualification; recover 8/10 key details and clearly identify uncertainty |
| Speaking | An 8-minute recommendation plus challenged discussion | Deliver a coherent persuasive case, answer four substantive challenges, acknowledge a valid objection and reformulate for a non-specialist without losing accuracy |
| Reading | Two dense related texts plus a brief formal/literary extract, about 2,300–3,200 Korean characters total | Synthesize agreement/conflict, distinguish rhetorical from literal assertions, explain register and identify at least two limits to the evidence |
| Writing | A 900–1,300-character synthesis/report plus a 150–250-character public-facing summary | Integrate rather than concatenate sources, attribute claims, address a counterargument, calibrate causal language and maintain distinct registers |

Apply the common five dimensions and 0–4 anchors. At score 3, language errors
are occasional and do not obscure source, scope, implication or interpersonal
stance. A 4 shows precise transfer to an unfamiliar variation and transparent
revision when an argument is challenged. Require 3 in every dimension on every
skill task and a delayed parallel assessment. These thresholds are local,
adaptable teaching criteria, **not official TOPIK pass marks**.

After completion, maintain reading/listening across domains, periodic writing
review and professional feedback. Any specialist use with real-world
consequences needs domain-qualified review in addition to language competence.
