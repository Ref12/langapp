# Stage 5 — systematic reports and supported public arguments

**Version:** 2026-09-07. **Exam orientation:** TOPIK II, award level 5.
**Educational reference:** NIKL Standard Curriculum 2020-54 level 5,
paraphrased: general social and work/study discourse, some professional texts,
systematic information delivery, discussion and supported opinions.
The official educational anchor, official test structure and this original
classroom assessment are distinct. See `../sources.json`.

## Prerequisites

Stage 4 outcomes: compare alternatives, qualify claims, distinguish sources,
interpret discourse relations and write a cohesive report. Diagnose with an
unseen discussion and a reasoned response to two conflicting accounts.
Verify fluent Hangul reading, precise quotation and stable spoken/written
register before increasing lexical density.

## Communicative outcomes

- **Listening:** follow extended social/work/study discourse and recover the
  hierarchy of claim, reason, example, limitation and recommendation.
- **Speaking:** give a structured report, defend a recommendation, handle
  objections and calibrate certainty without sounding evasive.
- **Reading:** synthesize related social and introductory professional texts;
  identify scope, implied evaluation, counterfactual reasoning and limitations.
- **Writing:** produce an organized evidence-based discussion or report that
  separates factual support from interpretation and acknowledges alternatives.
- **Pragmatics:** choose register for a briefing, collegial discussion or
  public statement; interpret dismissiveness and rhetorical exaggeration.

## Practice, revision and review

Build an evidence table, a meeting briefing, an interview summary, a policy
option comparison and a revised argumentative report. Use original or licensed
texts with complete English tutor equivalents. Identify invented examples or
statistics as hypothetical. Require a speaker to distinguish a generally
plausible tendency from a supported claim about a specific group.

Cycle new entries through receptive recognition, productive retrieval,
collocation and contextual transfer. Space retrieval at 1/3/7/14/30 days and
include a portfolio checkpoint after blocks 3, 6, 9 and 12. Revision must improve
meaning and organization, not simply insert more formal endings. Practice
signposting, prominence, pausing and repair in extended speech; maintain
ordinary Korean sound processes without manufacturing an artificial “formal”
pronunciation.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

| Skill | Task | Evidence for score 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | An unseen 3–4-minute briefing and a short response | Recover argument structure and at least 8/10 details; separate evidence, assumption and counterargument; note one limitation |
| Speaking | A 6-minute report with a 3-minute question period | State purpose, organize supporting points, compare alternatives and respond to objections with calibrated certainty and appropriate register |
| Reading | Two related social/professional texts totaling about 1,600–2,200 Korean characters | Synthesize shared and conflicting claims; identify source, scope, presupposition and evidence gaps without inventing agreement |
| Writing | A 650–900-character report or argument plus revision note | Give thesis, organized evidence, counterpoint, bounded recommendation and cohesive transitions; revision identifies and repairs one substantive weakness |

Apply the five-dimensional 0–4 rubric in `../README.md`. At score 3, errors do
not distort the hierarchy of argument or social stance; unfamiliar terms can be
paraphrased accurately. A 4 adapts to a new audience and revises a conclusion
after contrary evidence. Require at least 3 in every dimension on every skill,
then repeat a parallel task a week later. These local criteria do not promise
a TOPIK level, native-like mastery or unrestricted professional competence.
