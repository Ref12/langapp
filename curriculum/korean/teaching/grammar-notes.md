# Korean grammar placement and teaching judgments

All local placements, explanations, additions, and examples are original
AI-authored material requiring qualified Korean linguistic review. They are
not an official TOPIK grammar mapping, a calibrated assessment, or evidence
of examination readiness. The six original TOPIK files and their 216 IDs
remain unchanged; their labels identify reference provenance only.

## How to use the selection

The table below explicitly classifies every original construct: **195 selected
core**, **15 optional**, and **6 reference variants**. Nine separately identified
local additions address missing explicit familiar/plain endings and inflection
instruction. `authoring/teaching/grammar.yaml` therefore contains 219 placements:
204 numeric core placements and 15 branch placements. A reference variant is
preserved and available for a glossed comparison, not silently deleted or made
an additional required mastery item.

Placement is a communicative and prerequisite judgment, not an automatic
conversion from TOPIK band, topic, alphabetical order, or a quota. For example,
the early need to discuss an expectation brings `ko-topik5-g032` to level 10;
the uncommon literary forms remain optional even though learners can express
concession much earlier. Topic labels indicate useful practice contexts, not
level-generating rules. Phase targets are planning scales, not targets to fill
with rare words.

Modules introduce at most 25 senses and 3 constructions across adaptive
lessons of roughly 5–8 new senses. Reduce introduction when decoding,
inflection, or retrieval is fragile. Constructions at one level can span many
modules: the level-12 quotation family is not one five-construction lesson.
`construction-dependencies.yaml` maps each placed grammar ID to known or
explicitly co-taught prerequisites. Same-level dependencies mean ordered
co-teaching, never circular justification or instant mastery. A prerequisite
is a teaching support, not a requirement to reach perfect mastery before
communicating.

Early primitive constructions must not pull in whole contrast lessons as
mandatory component prerequisites. Subject, object, and topic particles do
not derive from one another or from the copula. Counting does not require
object marking; means -(으)로 does not require destination 에. Subject
honorification does not derive from formal listener speech. The ability
chunk -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 introduces its own bound form and does not require
either 못 or the different future chunk -(으)ㄹ 거예요.
Likewise, present -지 않아요 does not require either the alternative
negator 안 or past tense, and 못 does not morphologically derive from 안.
Teach those meaning contrasts when relevant rather than importing them as
undeclared constructions. Permission -아/어도 되다 introduces its own
fixed auxiliary relation; a helpful-action request is not its prerequisite.

Accordingly, a separate tourist route can introduce polite -아요/어요/해요,
subject -(으)시-, and helpful-action -아/어 주세요 together without
importing formal speech or the full core sequence. 감사해요 and 죄송해요
use the polite ending, not subject honorification; do not attach 시 to
first-person gratitude or apology merely because it is in that module.
An honorific greeting such as 안녕하세요 has a different analysis and
must be accounted for in its own realization.

The formal-ending item is not universally gated by the ㄹ-stem addition:
감사합니다 and 죄송합니다 do not use a ㄹ stem. A realization such as
삽니다 does need the relevant stem-and-ending instruction or an explicitly
recorded gloss. Similarly, -세요 directives introduce their fixed directive
form before the full level-8 subject/listener/beneficiary comparison. The
construction graph does not remove stem-specific or lexical obligations
from actual phrases. Route-level component closure must use the actual
realization, not infer mastery from a shared ending or a suggested core level.

Levels 26 and 30 deliberately synthesize earlier constructions rather than
invent new grammar to fill every level. Revisit plain narration at 26,
degree and threshold readings at 27–28, and evidence, attribution, quotation,
and register across the independent level-30 project. Literary interpretation
does not require every marked literary ending to become productive.

## Anchors, fixed forms, and lexical evidence

`anchors` are exact vocabulary-sense prerequisites, not word segmentation,
not a list of every word in an example, and not synonyms for the construction.
IDs `ko-nikl-NNNNN-sNNN` refer to original CSV records and definition-list
positions in the local registry, **not official NIKL sense IDs**.

Anchors are required lexical bases in both core and standalone sequences.
They must not collect every example word or alternative into a universal
prerequisite. The authored choices therefore distinguish:

| Selected tourist realization | What must not be imported automatically |
| --- | --- |
| 한 잔 | Other practice counters 개/명 or the unused cardinal forms 하나/둘. The counter construction owns the number-plus-unit relation, not a mandatory list of example lexemes; the actual 한 and counter 잔 need verified IDs. |
| N에 있어요 | The absent negative alternative 없다. The selected positive locative 있다 sense is the lexical base; the alternate predicate is introduced when used. |
| N에 가 주세요 | The unused come-verb 오다. The selected go-verb is the lexical base for destination marking; its viewpoint counterpart is introduced when used. |

Use the same authored anchor map for both routes; no validator exemption is
needed. Removing a surplus anchor does not erase a lexical distinction:
actual phrase vocabulary and surface realizations still require their own
verified identities. Narrower practice does **not** award mastery of the
untaught negative, alternate motion verb, other counter, or entire
construction family. Any form-changing operation still needs explicit
instruction or a recorded scaffold.

- Literal locative `ko-topik1-g006` anchors `ko-nikl-11948-s008` for the
  presence of a thing at a location. The corresponding absence sense
  `ko-nikl-46845-s007` remains useful core vocabulary but is not required by
  a positive locative phrase. Person-location examples need their own
  known or glossed vocabulary; none of these proves all uses of 있다/없다.
- The destination and motion-purpose items anchor the move sense
  `ko-nikl-25603-s001`. The alternate come sense `ko-nikl-48128-s001`
  remains useful core vocabulary and viewpoint practice, not an additional
  universal prerequisite for either construction.
- Comparison uses `ko-nikl-17430-s002`, comparative 더 rather than its
  additive sense. Superlatives anchor the adverb `ko-nikl-25966-s001`,
  not the unrelated noun 가장. The alternative adverb `ko-nikl-14944-s001`
  is separately selected when practicing 제일, not required merely to use
  the familiar 가장 pattern.
- The counter item owns a variable number-plus-counter structure, so no
  particular numeral or counter is a universal lexical anchor. Useful core
  practice includes `ko-nikl-23787-s001` and `ko-nikl-18823-s001`, plus
  bound units `ko-nikl-26923-s001` and `ko-nikl-37199-s001`. A phrase using
  한 잔 instead supplies its verified attributive numeral and cup-counter
  senses. 개 meaning dog and 명 meaning name are not substitutes;
  individual counters remain separate lexical items.
- Local inflection items anchor the actual illustrated stem senses. In
  particular, `ko-nikl-34748-s001` is 낫다 meaning recover,
  `ko-nikl-03918-s001` is 쓰다 meaning write, and
  `ko-nikl-08832-s010` is 살다 meaning reside. Their homographs or other
  meanings are not awarded by successful conjugation.

Most placements introduce a grammatical ending, particle, auxiliary, or fixed
bound expression under the grammar ID itself. Empty anchors are justified for
these items, rather than being a claim that the surrounding vocabulary is
already known. In particular:

| Grammar-owned family | Why not substitute a free lexical sense |
| --- | --- |
| Copular endings, particles, tense, politeness, subject honorific morphology | A suffix or particle is not an unrelated free noun or verb. |
| -아/어 주세요 and -아/어 주다/드리다 | Benefactive auxiliary use is not the independent act of handing over an object. |
| -아/어 보다, -나 보다, -ㄹ까 봐 | Trial, inference, and apprehension do not award a visual-perception sense of 보다. |
| -고 있다 and -아/어 있다 | Aspectual use is distinct from the selected literal location senses. |
| -ㄹ 수, -는 것, -ㄹ 줄, -ㄴ 적, -는 셈 | These bound constructions own their grammatical function, not every concrete sense of the written component. |
| Quotation with 하다 and formal causal/evaluative chunks | Learn the whole reported or discourse function; do not award an unrelated do-verb, wind, law, or physical-end sense. |
| Suffix passive patterns | Learn eligible lexical verbs and participant structure; suffix recognition does not prove an unanchored verb's meaning. |
| Number-plus-counter structure | The construction relates two lexical slots; actual numbers and counters are verified in each realization, not inferred from mastering the relation. |

All other example vocabulary must be already known or explicitly glossed;
record glosses and form tables as assistance. Some contrast examples in the
notes intentionally preview later endings. They explain a contrast, not a
requirement for independent early production. No lexical anchor is invented
for a missing constructional sense.

## Essential contrasts and revisits

### Particles and predicate types

Teach 은/는 as topic or contrast and 이/가 as subject marking, with context
showing why new identification or contrast matters. Do not describe 은/는 as
a universal subject marker or assume every sentence needs an overt pronoun.
Use 을/를 for objects, 에 for the selected existence/destination/time use,
and 에서 for action location or the independently taught route origin.
Particle omission in conversation is not a license to erase these contrasts
from controlled tasks.

Noun predicates use 이에요/예요; descriptive predicates are already
predicates: 방이 작아요, not a descriptive stem plus a redundant copula.
The negative noun predicate is 학생이 아니에요; 안 학생이에요 is not
the beginner replacement. Practice 이다/아니다 separately from 있다/없다.

Do not fill a missing lexical predicate by attaching 하다 to any convenient
noun. Where a verified action noun and the appropriate independent 하다
sense are available, an explicit phrase such as 공부를 해요 or 주문을 해요
can use object marking `ko-topik1-g005` and the polite predicate ending
`ko-topik1-g016`. This does not automatically create a source entry or
independent lexical identity for 공부하다 or 주문하다. A joined form needs
a reviewed, explicitly documented realization with the correct components;
the -해요 ending alone does not establish productive noun-to-predicate
derivation. In particular, do not assume that 미안하다, 피곤하다, and
필요하다 are freely assembled from interchangeable noun meanings.
Use an available source-backed expression, an explicitly glossed example,
or a separately reviewed support entry instead of inventing a sense ID.

The current support registry now directly retains the original unbanded
공부하다, 일하다, 시작하다, 주문하다, 필요하다, 부탁하다, 대답하다,
사랑하다, 행복하다, 미안하다, and 피곤하다 entries. They have their own
source sense IDs and exact official citation matches; this does not make an
arbitrary new noun-plus-하다 combination a sourced lexical entry.

Present action modifiers use -는, descriptive modifiers -(으)ㄴ, and
completed action modifiers -(으)ㄴ. The matching surface ending does not mean
the same predicate type or time. Existential families use 있는/없는 and
있는데/없는데; this includes 재미있는 and 맛있는. Later distinguish
읽던 from 읽었던 using an explicit unfinished/habitual versus bounded
past context, without claiming they never overlap.

### Requests, negatives, quantities, and respect

Start with helpful-action repair requests, then contrast 안, 못, -지 않다,
-지 못하다, -지 마세요, and -(으)면 안 되다. Unwillingness, inability,
prohibition, and a denied noun identification are different communicative
claims. In a form such as 공부를 안 해요, negation precedes the light verb;
do not force 안 before every written compound indiscriminately.

Native number forms contract before suitable counters: 하나/둘/셋/넷 give
한/두/세/네, and 스물 gives 스무 in combinations such as 스무 명.
Contrast native hours with Sino-Korean minutes and payment amounts in won.
Counter choice is lexical and contextual, not one universal number system.
Introduce two familiar counters before adding more; counters and number
forms do not create fictitious additional free-lemma mastery.

At level 8, explicitly separate:

1. **Listener politeness:** 해요 versus 합니다 addresses the listener.
2. **Subject honorification:** -(으)시- honors the grammatical subject.
3. **Humble reference:** 드리다 or 뵙다 lowers the actor's action in
   relation to a respected recipient or referent; it does not honor the actor.

Compare 선생님이 오셨어요 with 제가 선생님께 설명해 드렸어요.
The first honors the subject; the second does not make 저 an honored subject.
Teach 계시다, 주무시다, 드시다, and other lexical alternatives only with
their appropriate meanings and contexts, not by mechanically inserting 시
into every verb. Noun honorifics and family terms require lexical evidence.
-(으)세요 can describe an honored subject or perform a directive; context
and, when available, actual audio distinguish the use. -ㅂ시다 is not
automatically a deferential proposal to every superior.

Familiar 해체 requires a suitable relationship, not merely perceived age.
Do not strip 요 as a universal politeness conversion. Plain written
읽는다 contrasts with descriptive 좋다 and noun 학생이다; the dictionary
form 읽다 is not the ordinary present action declaration. For a familiar
proposal, explicitly gloss -자 when comparing the report -자고 하다.
Source quotation constructs introduce their own question and directive
quoted forms; they do not license blunt direct speech to strangers.

### Time, aspect, clause roles, and reported evidence

Past -았/었- is not a guarantee of completed perfective aspect in every
context. -(으)ㄹ 거예요 can express planning or expectation; -겠- varies
with person and situation. Contrast an activity in progress, a resulting
state, and a deliberately preserved result: 읽고 있어요, 문이 열려
있어요, 문을 열어 놓았어요. Clothing/posture uses such as 입고 있다
need lexical and aspectual explanation rather than a blanket “always ongoing”
rule. -아/어 두다 highlights preparation or retained usefulness beyond the
basic leave-result construction.

Differentiate same-subject simultaneity with -(으)면서 from the temporal
span -는 동안, which permits different subjects. Distinguish -다가 from
-았/었다가, -고 from an asserted cause, and causal -아/어서 from
-(으)니까 when the next clause is a suggestion or directive. Teach the
ordinary beginner restriction on causal -아/어서 as a useful initial
contrast, not an unqualified ban on every contextual imperative sequence.
The sequential -아/어서 use is a later contextual revisit, not evidence
that the causal reading disappeared.

Keep reported statements, questions, directives, and suggestions distinct:
온다고, 좋다고, 학생이라고, 오냐고, 오라고, 오자고. Preserve tense
and participant reference rather than reproducing the original listener
politeness ending mechanically. Question allomorphs and predicate-type
differences need reviewed examples; the compact -냐고 entry is not a claim
that every longer form is ungrammatical.

Personal observation (-더라고요), remembered hearsay (-다고 하던데),
attribution (N에 따르면), evidence-based inference, and rhetorical certainty
are different evidence positions. A convincing-sounding -ㄹ 리가 없다 or
-ㄹ 법하다 is not factual proof. -다고 해서 … 것은 아니다 rejects a
particular inference, not necessarily the premise itself or every instance of
the conclusion. -느냐에 따라 and -느냐에 달려 있다 embed a question
whose answer determines variation or outcome.

### Causative, passive, and nominalized prose

Under `ko-topik3-g025`, identify who causes or permits an action and who
performs it. -게 하다 is a periphrastic causative; it can mean permission
or compulsion depending on participants and context. Lexical causatives
such as 먹이다 and 보이다 must be taught with their actual lexical
meaning and argument pattern. Their suffixes are not freely attachable
instructions. The suffix inventory seen in lexical causatives can include
이/히/리/기/우/구/추; selection is lexical, not determined by the final
letter alone.

Under `ko-topik3-g026`, contrast an active object with a passive subject:
문을 열었어요 versus 문이 열렸어요. Korean often chooses an intransitive
or active expression where English uses a passive. Suffix passive
이/히/리/기 and causative suffixes can overlap in spelling without sharing
valency or meaning. A form such as 보이다 requires contextual lexical
disambiguation. Do not generate a suffix passive for every verb or combine
passive markers mechanically. The changing-quality -아/어지다 entry is
not permission to treat all such forms as passives.

Contrast nominalizing -기, clause-level 것, formal -(으)ㅁ, and a
relative clause modifying a concrete noun. Later distinguish causal
-(으)므로 from means -(으)ㅁ으로써: similar spelling does not make a
reason identical to a method. Spaced bound noun 바 and attached connective
-는바 have separate entries and functions. Literary and institutional
chunks are interpreted in context and may be replaced by plainer wording.

## Inflection families: learn the stem and the ending together

These are teaching families, not blanket final-letter rules. Keep a citation
form, a useful inflected form, and a contrastive retained-stem form together.
Revisit with past, modifiers, honorifics, and register as those functions
become available. Spelling instruction and pronunciation evidence remain
separate; no notes here supply audio.

| Local item | Productive contrast | Counterexample or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ko-teaching-g003` — ㄷ | 듣다 → 들어요, 들으세요, but 듣고; 걷다 “walk” → 걸어요; 묻다 “ask” → 물어요 | 닫다 → 닫아요; 묻다 “bury” → 묻어요. A shared spelling does not determine the lexical family. |
| `ko-teaching-g004` — ㅂ | 춥다 → 추워요/추운; 돕다 → 도와요/도운 but 돕고/돕는; 곱다 → 고와요/고운 | 입다 → 입어요, 잡다 → 잡아요, 좁다 → 좁아요. Distinguish action present 돕는 from completed 도운. |
| `ko-teaching-g005` — ㅅ | 낫다 “recover” → 나아요/나으면 but 낫고; 짓다 → 지어요; 붓다 → 부어요 | 씻다 → 씻어요, 웃다 → 웃어요, 벗다 → 벗어요. Retain the spelling 나아요; not 나요. |
| `ko-teaching-g006` — ㅎ | 그렇다 → 그래요/그런; 파랗다 → 파래요/파란; retain 그렇고 | 좋다 → 좋아요/좋은; 많다 → 많아요/많은. Do not remove every written ㅎ or predict vowels from the final letter. |
| `ko-teaching-g007` — 르 | 모르다 → 몰라요 but 모르고; 빠르다 → 빨라요; 부르다 → 불러요 | 따르다 → 따라요; 이르다 “reach” → 이르러요, but 이르다 “early” → 일러요. Different lexical families remain distinct. |
| `ko-teaching-g008` — ㅡ | 바쁘다 → 바빠요, 예쁘다 → 예뻐요; 쓰다 → 써요, 크다 → 커요; retain 바쁘고/쓰지 | Stem-final ㅡ changes before the relevant 아/어 ending, not before every suffix. Do not overwrite 르/러 families or confuse 푸다 → 퍼요 with this stem class. |
| `ko-teaching-g009` — ㄹ | 살다 → 살아요/살고/살지 but 사는/사세요/삽니다; 열다 → 열어요/여세요 | No deletion before all consonants. Prospective 살 거예요 has no extra doubled ㄹ; noun-final ㄹ plus 로 is a particle rule, not a verb-conjugation exception. |

Introduce regular control items too, so learners must choose rather than
apply a demonstrated transformation indiscriminately. An unknown
counterexample can be explicitly glossed for comparison; do not award its
vocabulary sense merely for identifying the inflection. In independent
tasks, use already established words and change the ending or relationship.

professional follows level 18, and literary follows level 24. Technical and
scientific branches are primarily receptive vocabulary/source tasks and do
not need invented grammar requirements merely to populate every branch.

## Review and evidence boundaries

The original reference notes and compact templates are not a complete grammar.
A qualified reviewer should check acceptability, predicate restrictions,
lexical alternations, reference, spacing, and interpersonal appropriateness
in each actual task. New examples need review too; being newly authored does
not make them validated.

Reading and listening evidence is receptive; typed, spoken, and handwritten
responses provide separate productive evidence under `mastery.yaml`.
Listening and spoken-production claims require actual audio and suitable
evaluation. Neither these files, a pronunciation spelling, a completed
module, nor a source band supplies that evidence automatically.
