# Hangul preparation — before level 1, not level 0

This unnumbered prerequisite is original AI-authored guidance and requires
qualified Korean linguistic review. It is not a reading-age standard, an
official examination prerequisite, or a claim about acquired pronunciation.
No character, stroke-order, or handwriting assets are supplied here.

## Entry tasks

- Recognize consonant and vowel letters within syllable blocks. Reassemble
  a short familiar word from its letters without substituting romanization.
- Distinguish the initial, medial, and optional final positions in a block.
  Notice that initial ㅇ is a placeholder while final ㅇ represents a sound.
- Read a few familiar words and a short spaced phrase slowly, checking the
  intended word rather than naming isolated letters alone. Keyboard input
  practice can support communication but does not establish handwriting.
- Notice that written forms and contextual pronunciation need not match
  letter by letter. Explore final-consonant realization and linking to a
  following vowel with reviewed examples; do not infer blanket sound rules
  from one word or promise pronunciation from a text-only demonstration.
- Locate a dictionary citation form ending in 다 and distinguish it from a
  polite conversational form. Full conjugation is taught through numbered
  construction items, not demanded as a prerequisite.

Use short sessions with familiar names and useful everyday words. Provide
clear type, enlargement, extra time, or another accessible response channel
as appropriate. Keep the assistance visible in the evidence record.

## Readiness and continued support

A learner can begin level 1 when they can identify the syllable blocks in
several previously practiced words, attempt a new short combination, and
request clarification. Record reading and any typed response separately.
Continue scaffolded Hangul work alongside level 1 if decoding remains slow;
do not make speed, penmanship, or perfect spelling a gate to interaction.

With actual suitable audio, a separate task may ask the learner to match a
heard familiar word to written alternatives. Without audio, mark listening
and spoken production unassessed. Hearing a model once is not evidence of
production, and a Hangul pronunciation line is not a recording.

Return to spacing, particle attachment, stem alternations, and contextual
pronunciation in later lessons. Preserve written distinctions even when
their pronunciation converges. `grammar-notes.md` supplies family-specific
inflection contrasts; those notes supplement, rather than replace,
qualified review and contextual practice.
