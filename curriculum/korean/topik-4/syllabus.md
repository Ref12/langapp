# Stage 4 — social issues, evidence and everyday workplace discourse

**Version:** 2026-09-07. **Exam orientation:** TOPIK II, award level 4.
**Educational reference:** NIKL Standard Curriculum 2020-54 level 4,
paraphrased: familiar social/abstract topics and basic workplace discourse,
including agreement, disagreement, reporting and interpretation of intentions.
This original curriculum and local rubric do not reproduce TOPIK's marking
scheme. Refer to `../sources.yaml` for official reference links.

## Prerequisites and diagnostic

Stage 3 outcomes, including indirect quotation, experience, cause/sequence,
modification, passives and supported recommendations. Diagnose with a reported
incident, a short social article and a 2-paragraph explanation. Check whether
the learner distinguishes what was seen, inferred and heard from someone else.
Retain fluent Hangul decoding, spacing and register control as prerequisites.

## Communicative outcomes

- **Listening:** follow a familiar news-style item, workplace discussion or
  narrative and separate observation, hearsay, expectation and evaluation.
- **Speaking:** present a comparison, discuss benefits and limitations,
  negotiate a plan and make a tactful recommendation or disagreement.
- **Reading:** interpret familiar social/abstract arguments, supporting
  examples, contrast, degree and source attribution.
- **Writing:** organize a comparison or problem/solution report with evidence,
  paragraph cohesion, a qualified conclusion and appropriate written register.
- **Pragmatics:** avoid presenting assumptions as facts; identify when an
  explanation sounds defensive, blaming or overly directive.

## Practice and cumulative review

Use paired accounts with conflicting evidence, meeting summaries, a comparison
of two housing/work options, a hypothetical survey chart and a short
problem/solution report. Label invented chart data as fictional. Require source
attribution in both Korean tutor material and its English equivalent; never
invent a real study. Ask for a plain-language paraphrase of each formal claim.

Review lexical IDs and grammar at 1/3/7/14/30 days. Every third block includes
an unseen listening item, an interaction and a revised paragraph using older
forms. Track repeated omissions, unsupported causal claims, quotation shifts
and spelling of similar-sounding connective forms. Pronunciation work now
emphasizes focus, thought groups, natural contractions and comprehensible
formal presentation, while still correcting meaning-changing sound errors.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

| Skill | Task | Evidence for score 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | A 2–3-minute familiar social or workplace discussion | Summarize the issue, positions and evidence; identify at least two stance/source distinctions and 8/10 key details |
| Speaking | A 5-minute comparison/recommendation plus three follow-ups | Give two criteria, a benefit and limitation for each option, a qualified recommendation and tactful responses to disagreement |
| Reading | A familiar social argument and a workplace notice totaling about 1,100–1,500 Korean characters | Reconstruct main claim, paragraph roles, contrast and cited source; distinguish an inference from an explicit assertion |
| Writing | A 450–650-character comparison or problem/solution report | Use an opening purpose, organized evidence and a bounded conclusion; maintain written register and explain rather than merely list facts |

Use the common five dimensions and 0–4 anchors. A 3 in form means that complex
clauses preserve causality, reference and stance despite minor errors; a 3 in
organization means each paragraph advances the argument. A 4 transfers the
argument to another audience and revises it when a premise changes. Require
3 in every dimension for every skill task and a delayed parallel assessment.
Repair the failed reasoning or discourse function before assigning more
advanced-looking endings.
