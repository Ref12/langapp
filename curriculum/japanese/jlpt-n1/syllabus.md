# JLPT N1-oriented syllabus: complex discourse and nuanced interpretation

## Standard, entry profile and limits

The [official JLPT competence summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) is the reference for advanced comprehension across a broad range of reading and listening. This original syllabus emphasizes complex argument, implicit stance, qualified inference, formal institutional language and recognition of selected literary constructions.

Prerequisite: N2 local receptive exit criteria, reliable contextual reading of common compounds, natural-speed comprehension of longer connected speech, and control of discourse relations. Diagnose with two 600–1,000-character texts, a three-minute discussion and 40 mixed prerequisite lexical prompts. Remediate failures of actor tracking, reference, negation and evidence before adding rare formal forms.

**Inventory:** 3,100 new lexeme-reading entries, 7,828 cumulative entries and 45 original constructs. The underlying 8,293 mapping rows were reduced transparently for overlap and unverifiable data; see the language README. Vocabulary labels are historical community estimates, not new official lists. The [official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html) explains the absence of exhaustive modern word/kanji/grammar specifications.

These 45 constructs are a **major-construct teaching selection**, not complete N1 grammar coverage. Advanced proficiency also requires depth in ordinary forms, idioms, collocations, genre, pragmatic inference and extensive reading/listening. Do not teach literary grammar as default everyday speech. Speaking and writing are enrichment, not JLPT-tested components.

## Outcomes

- Read 900–1,600-character original essays, reports, qualified arguments and selected literary-style narratives, identifying thesis, development, reference, implication and rhetorical effect.
- Separate what is asserted, presupposed, inferred, conceded or merely possible; cite the evidence supporting an interpretation.
- Compare two positions without erasing qualifications or attributing the tutor's assumptions to an author.
- Follow 3–5-minute natural-speed discussions or explanatory talks, tracking stance, logical relationships, implicit purpose and the final conclusion.
- Recognize formal/literary expressions in context and explain their force in English; choose simpler natural forms for ordinary interaction when appropriate.
- For **productive enrichment**, deliver a 5–7-minute evidence-based synthesis and write a 600–900-character structured response with a faithful English meaning equivalent.

Lengths, thresholds and script ranges are local teaching aids, not official score criteria or guarantees of exam readiness.

## Kanji, pronunciation and lexical depth

Plan toward approximately **1,800–2,200 cumulative high-utility kanji in words**, adjusted to learner goals. This range is not an official N1 character inventory. Derive the sequence from actual vocabulary, text needs and learner errors, prioritizing common compounds before obscure variants. Teach word-specific readings and meaning, distinguish visually similar compounds and check dictionary restrictions; never equate recognizing a character with knowing every compound containing it.

Reading support should normally be selective for proper names, unusual compounds and newly introduced items. Maintain kana fluency and katakana recognition, including unfamiliar but transparent loanwords. Practise lookup, typing and reading across headings, narrative prose, tables and conversational transcripts. Pitch accent, native audio and a separate exhaustive kanji curriculum are not included.

Use three lexical lanes:

1. **Breadth:** one modern, task-relevant sense for each current-level row.
2. **Depth:** additional relevant senses, collocations and register for existing N5–N2 rows, recorded against their existing IDs.
3. **Recognition-only extension:** labeled rare, archaic, specialist or sensitive senses encountered in reading. Do not force these into casual production or count them as ordinary learner necessities.

Suggested first-pass row budgets across blocks: **344, 344, 344, 344, 344, 344, 344, 344, 348**. Each block spans many sessions. Introduce about 12–20 new rows per short session only when delayed comprehension remains stable; no completion date follows from the nine-block outline.

Topic labels are coarse first-sense heuristics. Select from the listed buckets, inspect English meanings and POS, and draw at least a quarter of each allocation from relevant `general-language` rows. Use meaningful residual words to design additional subtopics. If a topic bucket is short, use suitable unused rows from others rather than padding it. Track every ID as introduced, previously known or explicitly deferred with a reason; audit remaining IDs every three blocks and schedule catch-up cycles.

## Extended practice protocol

Each cycle combines language knowledge and discourse rather than teaching rare patterns in isolation:

1. **Retrieve:** 15 mixed vocabulary/sense items and four earlier grammar contrasts; include common N5–N2 expressions in advanced contexts.
2. **Notice register and force:** compare the two original bilingual examples, a plainer paraphrase and a near-miss. Explain who could naturally say each version, to whom, and why.
3. **Read and map:** mark thesis, evidence, concession, author evaluation, reference and conclusion. Ask for both a faithful English summary and cited Japanese support.
4. **Listen before text:** use an original discussion or authorized recording. On the first pass, map speaker positions; on the second, resolve qualifications and implied relationships. Show transcript and translation afterward.
5. **Contrast genres:** transform a formal notice into a natural spoken explanation while preserving requirements; transform a narrative into an objective event summary without inventing motives.
6. **Produce as enrichment:** deliver a recommendation or synthesis, respond to a challenge, and write a follow-up with a clear audience. Reward natural choices, not maximum density of formal grammar.
7. **Transfer and review:** test the same distinction on an unfamiliar topic, then record the error type and a learner-generated repair.

Full passages and audio are not bundled. Generate original task texts with accurate English equivalents and review them for language, register and factual accuracy; or use separately licensed sources with attribution. Use tutor speech or reviewed TTS when native recordings are unavailable and record that limitation. A reading score must never substitute for a listening score.

Review approximately after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, with longer checks after each block cluster. At least half of cumulative checkpoints should recycle prior levels or blocks in new contexts. If delayed accuracy drops below 80%, or repeated inference errors persist, reduce new vocabulary, shorten input and explicitly contrast the offending relation. Speed practice follows stable understanding.

## Local exit assessment

Use two independent task sets at least seven days apart. Unseen material should rely mainly on taught core senses while allowing a small number of inferable words with documented support.

1. **Lexical/script retrieval:** 80 current-level rows plus 30 prerequisite rows, sampled across POS and topics. Add twenty context, sense and register decisions. Do not test every rare dictionary meaning as mandatory knowledge.
2. **Grammar/discourse interpretation:** 35 fresh tasks covering at least 30 current IDs and important previous-level contrasts. Include accountability, degree of certainty, concession, scope, register, temporal structure and cause versus correlation.
3. **Reading:** three 900–1,600-character texts from different genres, including a qualified argument and a narrative or report. Ask twenty questions: main claim, supporting detail, reference, qualifier, inference and author stance.
4. **Listening:** three 3–5-minute natural-speed discussions or talks; twenty questions on speaker positions, reasoning, implicit purpose and conclusions. Allow at most two plays for the local proficiency check; record first-play performance separately.
5. **Speaking enrichment:** a 5–7-minute synthesis with five requirements: faithful representation of source positions, supporting evidence, explicit qualification, reasoned recommendation and response to a challenge.
6. **Writing enrichment:** a 600–900-character structured response with five requirements: clear thesis, accurate source representation, evidence, concession and coherent conclusion. Add a faithful English version for meaning verification.

## Observable local rubric

| Dimension | 0–1: major comprehension gap | 2: developing | 3: independently effective | 4: precise and transferable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary/script | Below 50%; repeated breakdown on established compounds | 50–79%, or depends heavily on glosses | At least 80% overall and prerequisites; at least sixteen of twenty context/register decisions | At least 90%; preserves fine sense and register distinctions on delayed transfer |
| Grammar/discourse | Below 50%; systematically misreads scope, force or agency | 50–79%; arrives at correct analysis with cues | At least 80%; explains major relation and register distinctions without cues | At least 90%; justifies rejection of plausible alternatives without adding unsupported assumptions |
| Reading | Fewer than ten of twenty evidence-supported answers | Ten to fifteen; some implicit relationships require guidance | At least sixteen; thesis and author stance correct in every text | At least eighteen; precise evidence for qualifications, references and implications |
| Listening | Fewer than ten of twenty after two plays | Ten to fifteen after two plays | At least sixteen; speaker positions and conclusions accurately tracked | At least eighteen with one play at the documented natural pace |
| Speaking enrichment | Source positions or intended recommendation largely unrecoverable; 0 for no attempt | Three of five requirements with prompts | Four of five; intelligible structure, appropriate qualification and register | All five; precise synthesis, natural interaction and independent repair |
| Writing enrichment | Thesis, source fidelity or organization largely absent | Three of five requirements; several material repairs needed | Four of five; argument coherent and source meaning preserved | All five; controlled nuance, clear organization and independent correction of high-impact errors |

Complete the local receptive course when vocabulary/script, grammar/discourse, reading and listening each reach 3 on both task sets. Speaking/writing are reported separately as enrichment. Do not infer an official JLPT result or a four-skill CEFR level; not all advanced ability is captured by a grammar inventory or these tasks. Mark missing audio assessment “not assessed.”

Record task/source version, date, pace, plays, support, IDs and sense numbers, raw scores, cited evidence, rubric ratings and error-driven next steps. Continue extensive reading/listening, pragmatic interaction and human feedback after the curriculum exit; the inventory is a foundation, not a limit on the language.
