# JLPT N3-oriented syllabus: independent everyday understanding

## Standard, placement and scope

The [official JLPT level summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) treats N3 as the bridge between basic learning and broader real-life understanding. This syllabus operationalizes that progression through connected everyday reading and listening, reasons, viewpoints and longer instructions. It is not a reproduction of official test specifications.

Prerequisite: N4 local receptive exit criteria, ordinary kana fluency, basic plain-form embedding, verb groups, conditional forms and giving/receiving perspective. Diagnose with a practical message, a 90-second exchange and 30 mixed N5/N4 vocabulary prompts. Require at least 80% comprehension before loading unfamiliar register or dense compounds; otherwise assign focused bridge lessons.

**Inventory:** 1,667 new lexeme-reading entries, 2,981 cumulative entries and 45 original constructs. Waller's vocabulary labels are community estimates; grammar placement, lesson sequencing, text lengths, script targets and rubrics are local teaching decisions. Modern JLPT does not publish an exhaustive vocabulary/grammar list; see the [official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html). JLPT does not assess speaking or writing.

## Outcomes

- Read everyday explanations, messages, short opinion pieces and instructions of roughly 300–600 Japanese characters, locating main points, reasons and temporal relationships.
- Follow a 90–150-second everyday exchange at a moderately natural pace, identifying purpose, the reason for a change and the agreed next step.
- Distinguish evidence, hearsay, inference, logical expectation and categorical denial.
- Track causative/passive roles, benefit direction and basic respectful/humble perspective.
- Recognize comparison, topic, cause, concession and change across a paragraph rather than only completing isolated sentences.
- For **productive enrichment**, discuss a familiar practical issue for 3–4 minutes and write a connected 250–400-character explanation with an accurate English meaning summary.

## Script and lexical depth

Plan toward approximately **550–750 cumulative high-utility kanji**, selected from actual target words rather than an asserted official N3 list. Prioritize compounds used in work, services, health, social life and everyday information. Teach word-level readings, common inflected forms, and recognition of phonetic/semantic components without presenting component guesses as verified pronunciations.

Use readings for genuinely new words in the initial lesson, then remove routine support from subsequent texts. Include kana-heavy authentic-style layouts and katakana terminology as well as kanji-rich passages. Practise typing, dictionary lookup and recognizing variant spellings. Do not require every JMdict sense or reading variant.

Vocabulary progression has two lanes:

1. **Breadth:** introduce one modern task-relevant sense for each of the 1,667 current IDs.
2. **Depth:** revisit N5/N4 rows with additional context, transitivity, collocations and register. Record the new sense against its existing ID rather than claiming a new word.

Suggested first-pass block budgets: **185, 185, 185, 185, 185, 185, 185, 185, 187**. A block therefore needs many micro-lessons. Introduce about 10–15 new rows per short session, reducing the rate when delayed recognition falls below 80%.

Topic tags are coarse first-sense heuristics. Filter by the topics named below, inspect English and POS, and select words genuinely useful for the task. Draw at least a quarter of each allocation from `general-language`; use actual meanings to assign them to the lesson. If a thematic bucket is short, fill from another relevant bucket. Track introduced, previously known and reasoned deferrals; audit all unused IDs in blocks 3, 6 and 9, and schedule catch-up cycles.

## Tutor practice cycle

Use 45–60-minute lessons or divide the following into shorter sessions:

- Retrieve 12 mixed current and prerequisite items, including two sense-choice prompts.
- Compare the supplied bilingual examples and one new, reviewed minimal contrast. For every generated Japanese sentence, keep an accurate English equivalent.
- Read a 150–300-character lead-in and annotate only main claim, reason, actor and time; gradually expand toward exit lengths.
- Listen to a new dialogue before showing text. Ask for the overall situation on the first play and relationships or decisions on the second. Reveal the transcript and English after answers.
- Role-play a practical problem with two constraints; then write a short follow-up. Record speaking/writing as enrichment, not JLPT marks.
- End with three unseen transfer items and one self-repaired sentence.

Use tutor speech, licensed audio or reviewed TTS; document pace and source. No bundled audio or ready-made full passage bank is included. Keep prose translation available for checking without exposing it during assessment.

Review after roughly 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. At each three-block checkpoint, half the material should draw on earlier blocks and N5/N4. Below 80% delayed recognition or repeated role/negation errors, reduce new-item load and teach the contrast with new vocabulary. Track meaning, form and register errors separately.

## Local exit tasks

Use two unseen, parallel task sets at least seven days apart:

1. **Vocabulary/script:** 60 current rows plus 20 prerequisite rows, stratified across topics and POS; test readings and appropriate core meanings. Include ten context-dependent sense selections.
2. **Grammar:** 30 interpretation tasks covering at least 25 current IDs and ten prerequisite contrasts. Required themes: participant roles, scope of negation, evidence versus inference, temporal sequence and causal stance.
3. **Reading:** three 300–600-character texts covering practical instructions, an explanation and a short opinion. Ask fifteen questions about main point, detail, reason, sequence and reference.
4. **Listening:** three 90–150-second moderately natural dialogues or announcements, at most two plays; fifteen questions, including each speaker's purpose and the final decision.
5. **Speaking enrichment:** a 3–4-minute discussion of a practical problem. Explain it, suggest alternatives, respond to a condition, distinguish certainty from inference, and repair a misunderstanding.
6. **Writing enrichment:** a 250–400-character message or explanation with five requirements: clear purpose, two reasons/details, a contrast or condition, coherent ordering, and suitable register. Provide an English equivalent for meaning verification.

## Observable local rubric

| Dimension | 0–1: substantial gap | 2: developing | 3: independent task performance | 4: reliable transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary/script | Below 50%; frequent familiar-reading breakdown | 50–79%, or relies on glosses for common words | At least 80% overall and on prerequisite rows; at least eight of ten sense selections | At least 90% with accurate contextual senses after a delay |
| Grammar and discourse | Below 50%; repeatedly reverses actor, cause or negation | 50–79%; interprets correctly after cues | At least 80%; identifies key scope and role distinctions without cues | At least 90%; explains a distractor's meaning change using evidence |
| Reading | Fewer than eight of fifteen supported answers | Eight to eleven with occasional support | At least twelve correct; main point and sequence correct in each text | At least fourteen correct with evidence for reference and implied links |
| Listening | Fewer than eight of fifteen after two plays | Eight to eleven correct after two plays | At least twelve correct, including final decisions and participant roles | At least fourteen correct with one play at the recorded pace |
| Speaking enrichment | Purpose largely unachieved; 0 for no attempt | Three of five functions with prompts | Four of five functions; connected intelligible speech and workable register | All five functions, clear qualification and independent repair |
| Writing enrichment | Purpose/organization largely missing | Three of five requirements met; several prompted repairs | Four of five; reasons and sequence clear despite occasional errors | All five; cohesive paragraphs, controlled stance and independently checked meaning |

Advance receptive study when vocabulary/script, grammar/discourse, reading and listening each reach 3 on both sets. A production strength cannot conceal a receptive gap. Productive results remain separate enrichment; absence of audio means listening is “not assessed,” not successful.

Store raw answers, correct/total, IDs and sense numbers, task version, date, timing, reading support, plays, rubric ratings, corrected examples and planned review. These are not scaled JLPT scores or an official proficiency certificate.
