# JLPT N2-oriented syllabus: broader information and argument

## Standard and admission

The [official JLPT competence summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) is the reference for broader everyday and public-domain reading/listening. Here the tutor develops comprehension of explanation, argument, institutional notices and natural-speed exchanges. Outcomes and rubrics are local teaching interpretations, not official task specifications or marks.

Prerequisite: N3 receptive exit criteria, fluent kana decoding, recognition of several hundred high-utility kanji in words, connected plain/polite discourse, basic honorific perspective and interpretation of common causes, conditions and inferences. Diagnose with a 400–600-character passage, a two-minute exchange, 30 prerequisite words and ten scope/role contrasts. Repair repeated negation, actor or evidence errors before advancing.

The level contains **1,747 new lexeme-reading entries**, **4,728 cumulative entries** and **45 original constructs**. Waller placements are community estimates. Grammar estimates may differ from other curricula; no exhaustive modern official word/grammar list exists, as discussed in the [official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html).

## Outcomes

- Read original informational and opinion texts of approximately 600–1,000 Japanese characters, extracting thesis, supporting evidence, exceptions and author stance.
- Interpret rules, service notices, comparative information and workplace explanations without treating every formal connector as interchangeable.
- Follow natural-speed, clearly articulated 2–3-minute discussions or explanatory talks, identifying purpose, logic, participant stance and the chosen action.
- Distinguish probability from certainty, possibility from risk, necessity from reluctance, and correlation from a justified causal claim.
- Recognize formal register and convert meaning into a plainer English explanation without erasing qualifications.
- As **productive enrichment**, give a 4–5-minute reasoned recommendation and write a 400–600-character explanation or professional message with a faithful English equivalent.

JLPT does not assess speaking or writing. All length, speed, kanji and mastery targets below are local planning aids, not official requirements.

## Script, register and vocabulary development

Work toward approximately **900–1,200 cumulative high-utility kanji**, selected from actual words in the curriculum. This is not an official N2 kanji list. Prioritize productive compounds in public life, employment, study, economics and science. Practise distinguishing visually similar characters, multiple word readings, inflection and compound structure. Never turn a guessed component reading into a verified vocabulary answer.

After initial instruction, omit readings for established words; supply selective support for unfamiliar names or specialist terms. Include headings, tables, notices and kana-heavy conversational transcripts so recognition does not depend on a single layout.

Use two progress measures: **new lexical breadth** in this CSV and **sense/register depth** for existing N5–N3 entries. Every row's dictionary senses are reference information; teach common, relevant senses before rare or technical ones. Identify register labels before allowing a word in an everyday role-play.

Suggested first-pass row budgets by block: **194, 194, 194, 194, 194, 194, 194, 194, 195**. A block spans many micro-lessons. Begin around 10–18 new rows per session and adjust to delayed recall and workload. Do not use these numbers as a fixed completion deadline.

Filter the named topic buckets, inspect English and POS, and include at least a quarter of each allocation from useful `general-language` rows. Tags are coarse first-sense heuristics, not validated domains. If a bucket is short, allocate suitable unused rows from another. Track every ID as introduced, previously known or specifically deferred. Audit residual IDs every third block and plan catch-up tasks, rather than claiming completion from a few representative words.

## Tutor practice protocol

Use a recurring cycle of:

1. **Delayed retrieval:** 12–15 mixed lexical/sense items and three discourse contrasts; include prerequisite material.
2. **Noticing:** compare the two original bilingual examples, then a new reviewed paraphrase. Identify register, scope and the logical connection rather than memorizing an English keyword alone.
3. **Reading:** annotate claim, support, exception, reference and connective function. Increase length only after accuracy is stable. Ask for evidence in the Japanese text and a faithful English explanation.
4. **Listening:** play an original talk or exchange before supplying its transcript. First identify purpose and participant positions; second resolve details and the outcome. Record the speech source, pace and number of plays.
5. **Production enrichment:** recommend a course of action with two reasons and a concession, then write a brief follow-up. Avoid stuffing five formal constructions into otherwise casual speech.
6. **Transfer:** replace the topic and test the same relation. Ask why a near-synonym or alternative connector would be wrong or less appropriate.

No audio corpus or full text bank is bundled. Use tutor speech, appropriately licensed audio or reviewed TTS. Every generated Japanese instructional passage requires an English equivalent and review of factual accuracy, register and level. Translations remain hidden during independent tasks.

Review around days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30; schedule a longer delayed check after each three-block cycle. At least 40% of checkpoint material should come from earlier blocks and levels. Below 80% recognition or repeated logical-scope errors, reduce new-item load, shorten texts and explicitly contrast the confusion. Increased speed must not conceal poor comprehension.

## Local exit tasks

Use two different task sets at least seven days apart:

1. **Lexical/script:** 70 current-level rows and 25 prerequisite rows; include fifteen contextual sense/register decisions. Sample across POS and topics instead of only familiar themes.
2. **Grammar/discourse:** 30 new interpretation tasks covering at least 25 current IDs plus prerequisite contrasts. Require role, negation, qualifier, concession, necessity and risk interpretation.
3. **Reading:** three 600–1,000-character pieces: an explanation, a qualified opinion and a procedural text with a small table. Ask fifteen main-point, detail, scope and inference questions.
4. **Listening:** three 2–3-minute natural-speed discussions or explanations, at most two plays; fifteen questions on position, reasons, changes and final actions.
5. **Speaking enrichment:** a 4–5-minute recommendation meeting five functions: problem definition, evidence, comparison, concession and a response to challenge.
6. **Writing enrichment:** a 400–600-character professional message or explanation with clear audience, thesis/purpose, evidence, qualification and conclusion; add an English equivalent for meaning verification.

## Observable local rubric

| Dimension | 0–1: substantial gap | 2: developing | 3: independent | 4: sustained transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary and script | Below 50%; familiar compounds frequently misread | 50–79%, or needs repeated gloss access | At least 80% overall and on prerequisites; at least twelve of fifteen contextual choices | At least 90%; handles changed contexts and preserves register distinctions after delay |
| Grammar and argument | Below 50%; repeated unsupported or reversed interpretation | 50–79%; corrects scope after prompts | At least 80%; distinguishes assertion, inference, risk and qualification | At least 90%; explains distractor errors with textual evidence |
| Reading | Fewer than eight of fifteen justified answers | Eight to eleven, with occasional support | At least twelve correct; thesis and restriction correctly identified in each text | At least fourteen with precise evidence and no unjustified generalization |
| Listening | Fewer than eight of fifteen after two plays | Eight to eleven after two plays | At least twelve; identifies each speaker's position and the final action | At least fourteen with one play at the recorded pace |
| Speaking enrichment | Recommendation not intelligibly established; 0 for no attempt | Three of five functions with prompting | Four of five; coherent reasons and suitable register | All five; responds to challenge and qualifies claims without prompting |
| Writing enrichment | Purpose or argument largely missing | Three of five requirements; important links need repair | Four of five; clear organization and recoverable qualification | All five; accurate audience/register and independent correction of high-impact errors |

Advance receptive study only when vocabulary/script, grammar/argument, reading and listening each reach 3 on both sets. Report speaking and writing separately; their results are not official JLPT marks and cannot establish an official pass. Unavailable listening is “not assessed.”

Log task versions, source/pace, date, duration, IDs/senses, support, plays, raw accuracy, evidence for inference judgments, rubric ratings and targeted review actions.
