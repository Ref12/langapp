# JLPT N4-oriented syllabus: connected everyday communication

## Standard and prerequisites

The [official JLPT level summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) is the reference for progressing through basic everyday reading and clearly delivered listening. The operational outcomes here are original paraphrases and teaching targets. The [official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html) confirms that exhaustive modern word/grammar lists are not published.

Prerequisite: the N5 local receptive exit criteria, comfortable decoding of both kana systems, and recognition of basic particle roles, verb groups, polite tense, te-forms and everyday requests. Begin with 20 N5 word prompts, ten tense/particle items, one short notice and one spoken exchange. Remediate any dimension below 80% before increasing the new-item load.

This level introduces **635 new lexeme-reading entries and 45 grammar constructs**, with a cumulative inventory of **1,314 entries**. Vocabulary placement is a Waller community estimate; grammar placement and all rubrics are original teaching choices. JLPT tests receptive skills and language knowledge, not the productive enrichment below.

## Outcomes

- Read connected everyday messages, brief narratives and instructions of approximately 150–300 Japanese characters with limited assistance on unfamiliar words.
- Follow a 60–90-second clearly delivered exchange about a plan, request or minor problem, separating the initial proposal from the final decision.
- Interpret plain embedded predicates, conditions, experience, reasons, change, benefit and simple passive/respectful constructions.
- Select the relevant English sense of familiar polysemous words from context rather than reciting every dictionary gloss.
- In **speaking enrichment**, sustain a 2–3-minute exchange, describe an experience, make a polite request and negotiate a plan.
- In **writing enrichment**, produce an 8–12-sentence account or message with a reason, sequence, condition and appropriate style.

Text length, speed, script targets and mastery thresholds are local aids, not official exam specifications.

## Kana, kanji and pronunciation

Consolidate automatic kana decoding, especially long vowels, small characters and katakana loanwords. Review verb sound changes through inflected forms in real words. Teach new kanji through the selected vocabulary readings, not independent character readings.

Plan toward roughly **250–350 cumulative high-utility kanji** across N5 and N4, depending on learner needs; no official kanji-list status is claimed. Prioritize directions, family, schooling, work, weather and frequent verbs. In blocks 1–3 supply readings for new words; in blocks 4–6 fade familiar readings; in blocks 7–9 provide selective support on genuinely new material. Include handwriting or typing recognition and dictionary lookup without demanding specialist calligraphy.

## Vocabulary coverage and pacing

Suggested first-pass budgets across nine blocks: **70, 70, 70, 70, 70, 70, 70, 70, 75** new rows. Introduce 8–12 rows per short session only while delayed recognition remains at least 80%. Review N5 in every block; N5 entries are not repeated in this level's CSV.

For each block, select rows from the named topics after reading the actual English gloss and POS; topic tags are coarse first-sense heuristics. Reserve at least a quarter of new selections for useful rows in `general-language`. A word's primary meaning may fit another topic better than its automatic tag. Move it to the appropriate lesson without changing its source.

Track every current-level ID as introduced, previously known or deferred with a specific reason. Cover one modern, task-relevant sense first; uncommon or specialist senses are reference material, not N4 requirements. If a theme bucket runs short, choose contextually suitable unused rows from another bucket. Audit unused IDs in blocks 6 and 9 and add lexical micro-cycles until the first-pass queue is complete.

## Practice and review

For a 35–50-minute session: start with ten delayed retrievals, compare two bilingual examples, practise one contrast, read a short task text, listen before seeing its transcript, then perform a role-play and write a short follow-up message.

Keep **meaning, form and social choice** separate in feedback. A grammatically possible request can still sound too direct; ask the learner to identify the relationship and burden before selecting wording. For giving/receiving constructions, diagram benefactor, beneficiary and speaker viewpoint. For conditionals, contrast intention, automatic result and contextual premise rather than translating every form as a single English “if.”

Use original transcripts with verified English equivalents. Tutor speech or reviewed TTS is acceptable with the source and pace recorded; no bundled audio is provided. Two plays precede transcript access. Have the learner shadow a short segment after comprehension, not instead of it.

Review on approximately days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30. Below 80% delayed recognition, reduce new words and practise the confused pair in a different task. Every third block uses a cumulative check containing at least 40% N5 material. Keep a record of which dictionary sense was taught.

## Local exit assessment

Administer two different task sets at least seven days apart:

1. **Vocabulary/script:** 50 current-level rows across topics plus 20 N5 rows; prompt selected meanings, readings and contextual choices, not rare senses.
2. **Grammar:** 25 fresh interpretation/selection items sampling at least 20 current IDs plus five N5 contrasts. Include reported versus apparent evidence, conditional meaning, obligation versus optionality, and giving/receiving perspective.
3. **Reading:** three original texts of approximately 150–300 characters: a narrative, practical instruction and scheduling message. Ask twelve purpose, sequence and detail questions.
4. **Listening:** three 60–90-second clear dialogues, at most two plays. Ask twelve questions covering the final decision, reason and participant roles.
5. **Speaking enrichment:** a 2–3-minute problem-solving exchange: explain the situation, make a request, respond to a condition, agree on a plan and repair one misunderstanding.
6. **Writing enrichment:** an 8–12-sentence message or narrative meeting five content requirements, with an English version to verify intended meaning. Maintain one appropriate politeness level unless quoting.

## Observable local rubric

| Dimension | 0–1: foundational gap | 2: developing with support | 3: independent | 4: consistent transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary/script | Below 50%; repeated decoding or core-meaning failure | 50–79% with occasional reading cues | At least 80% overall and on the N5 subset | At least 90% plus correct contextual sense choices after delay |
| Grammar | Below 50%; recurrent scope or participant reversal | 50–79%; can repair after a model | At least 80%, including benefit direction and obligation contrasts | At least 90%; explains why a plausible alternative changes meaning |
| Reading | Fewer than six of twelve answers supported by text | Six to nine correct with limited scaffolding | At least ten correct independently | At least eleven correct across a second text set |
| Listening | Fewer than six of twelve correct after two plays | Six to nine correct after two plays | At least ten correct; final plan and participants correctly identified | At least eleven correct with one play at the stated clear pace |
| Speaking enrichment | Purpose not achieved; 0 for no attempt | Three of five functions achieved with prompts | Four of five achieved with intelligible connected speech | All five achieved with an appropriate request and self-repair |
| Writing enrichment | Message largely incomplete or incoherent | Three of five requirements met; several prompted repairs | Four of five met; sequence and style are recoverable | All five met with clear connections and independently corrected high-impact errors |

Advance the receptive course when vocabulary/script, grammar, reading and listening each reach 3 on both task sets. Report productive skills separately and continue practice where weak; do not invent official JLPT speaking/writing marks or a guaranteed pass. Mark unavailable listening as “not assessed.”

Store task version, date, IDs and sense numbers sampled, support, plays, raw correct/total, ratings, corrected learner examples and next review actions.
