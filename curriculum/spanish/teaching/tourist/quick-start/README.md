# Spanish travel quick start

Use `sequence.yaml` directly: it introduces its own vocabulary and grammar,
with no assumed core course or other travel lessons. Its first 25 original
phrases are the same records used in the full travel route.

| Unit | Practical task |
| --- | --- |
| Polite repair | Greet, explain a misunderstanding, and ask for repetition or writing |
| Food basics | Request a drink, a menu, an ingredient omission, and the bill |
| Finding your way | Locate a restroom, distinguish left and right, and ask for a map |
| Paying | Ask a price, check payment options, and request a receipt |
| Asking for help | State a need, request human help, and explain a missing passport |

Begin with a few phrases, pointing or English support, and about 5-8 new
senses at a time. A unit is not one timed lesson. The first repair frames can
be practised without first studying a complete present-tense or clitic
paradigm. Broader constructions and their prerequisites are introduced only
when the later units need them.

Use `vocabulary.min.yaml`, `grammar.min.yaml`, and `phrases.min.yaml` as the
ordered local introductions. The sequence also contains full review records
and each phrase's explicit lexical, grammatical, written-form, and reading
links. Related senses are not interchangeable: a bill is not a count, cash is
not the adjective effective, and a restroom is not a bath.

For retrieval, hide an answer-revealing English hint and change a relevant
detail. Record the actual assistance and modality; a recited phrase does not
establish independent use of every component. IPA and text are not evidence
of listening or spoken pronunciation. Original readings use the documented
wordwise seseo/yeismo convention, not a universal accent or a recording.

Ask and confirm rather than assuming a payment method, ingredient request,
service, or facility is available. These phrases do not establish dietary
safety or replace medical, legal, or emergency assistance. Do not delay
urgent help for a language exercise.
