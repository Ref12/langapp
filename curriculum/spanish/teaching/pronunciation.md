# Spanish pronunciation: provenance and instructional choices

There is no single “neutral Spanish” pronunciation in this curriculum. A
lexical reading reports its source; an original travel-phrase reading uses a
declared instructional convention. Neither is evidence that a learner has
heard or produced the item successfully.

## Source lexical readings

Lexical `pr` retains sourced broad IPA variants, including stress, together
with supplied labels in the expanded reference record. Preserve meaningful
alternatives and their original qualifiers rather than blending their
segments into a reading that the source never supplied. The adapter documents
deterministic selection and display order; complete sourced alternatives and
reading provenance remain available in expanded data.

An unlabelled source variant is **source-unspecified**. Do not attach a
country, region, or “standard” status merely by interpreting its symbols.
Absence of a regional label is not evidence of geographic neutrality.
Distinguish broad phonemic readings from more detailed phonetic alternatives
when the source supplies both.

A selected lexical item without a suitable sourced reading needs an explicit
authored override with origin, convention, and human-review status, or must
be deferred. Spelling is not a fallback pronunciation. Do not silently
synthesize readings, remove stress, or label an authored guess as source IPA.
Inflected phrase surfaces require their own supported realization; an
infinitive's IPA does not pronounce a conjugation or an attached-clitic form.

## Original travel-phrase convention

Original travel readings use **broad pedagogical seseo plus yeísmo, with
lexical stress and careful full consonants**:

- Seseo models `s`, `z`, and `c` before `e/i` with /s/, rather than a
  /s/–/θ/ distinction.
- Yeísmo models `ll` and consonantal `y` with the same broad category, normally
  written /ʝ/ here. That symbol is a teaching convention, not a promise that
  every speaker uses the same phonetic realization.
- Retain consonants such as final /s/ and intervocalic /d/ in this careful
  model instead of silently introducing weakening or deletion. Ordinary
  speech may realize them differently.
- Show lexical stress in polysyllabic words and retain the intended vowel
  sequence. Broad transcription does not attempt to encode every phrase-level
  reduction, liaison, intonation contour, or contextual allophone.

This is an authored choice for consistent early practice, **not one
pan-Latin-American accent** and not a claim that other pronunciations are
less correct. Do not “correct” sourced lexical readings to this convention.
If another original phrase convention is offered, label it as a separate
alternative rather than silently mixing conventions inside a phrase.

## Practical listening and production priorities

- **Stress and written accents:** compare the different stress in `hablo`
  and `habló`. Written accent marks also distinguish forms such as `tú/tu`;
  a spelling contrast does not always create an extra phonemic contrast.
  Question words retain accents in indirect questions: `No sé dónde está`.
- **Vowels and hiatus:** keep vowel quality clear. Compare the vowel sequence
  in `país` with the diphthong in `paisaje`; spelling and stress help but do
  not replace listening. `U` can be silent in `que/qui` and `gue/gui`;
  `ü` in `vergüenza` signals pronunciation of that vowel.
- **Tap and trill:** distinguish single intervocalic `r` and `rr`, as in
  `pero/perro`; word-initial `r` has a different distribution from the
  intervocalic tap. Teach an attainable contrast with recorded models and
  feedback, not an English spelling imitation.
- **Silent letters and shared categories:** ordinary Spanish `h` is silent;
  `ch` is a digraph, not silent `h` added to an independent `c`. Do not impose
  an English /v/ for ordinary Spanish `v`; `b/v` spelling does not generally
  prescribe two separate phonemes.
- **Clitics and stress:** practise attached forms such as `dímelo` as complete
  stress-bearing words. Compare `no me lo diga`, whose pronouns are written
  before the verb. The abstract grammar template has no `pr`.
- **Punctuation and intonation:** opening `¿` and `¡` are required in the
  corresponding full written questions and exclamations. Actual question
  intonation depends on the question, stance, and variety; a question mark
  cannot stand in for a recording.

Examples in this note are original illustrations, not imported dictionary
readings and not an inventory of additional lexical overrides.

## Respectful recognition of variation

Seseo and yeísmo are widely used and accepted, as described in the reference
links below. Distinción differentiates /s/ and /θ/ in relevant spellings;
lleísmo preserves a distinction between `ll` and `y`. Both distinguishing
and merging patterns deserve recognition without turning a regional pattern
into an error diagnosis.

Some Rioplatense speech has postalveolar realizations associated with
yeísmo, often represented with [ʒ] or [ʃ]. Distribution depends on speakers,
settings, and local patterns; do not assign either sound to everyone from a
country or derive a source label that was not supplied. Likewise, consonant
aspiration, weakening, and deletion vary by context and speaker, not by one
blanket “Latin American” rule.

Use labelled recordings when teaching recognition, state whose speech is
being modelled when known, and distinguish the recording's documented
provenance from general background knowledge. A learner may need recognition
of several patterns without being required to imitate each one. Forms of
address and regional lexical choices are separate but related teaching
decisions; see `grammar-notes.md`.

## Evidence and review

Listening judgments require actual audio presented to the learner, not only
a transcript or IPA. Spoken-production judgments require actual learner
audio and suitable evaluation of the intended task, including intelligibility
and relevant contrasts. Typed answers establish neither. Keep performance
records separate by item and modality under `mastery.yaml`.

Original readings and pedagogy are AI-authored and require human
Spanish-language, pronunciation, and pedagogy review. No reviewed recording
inventory or learner observation is implied by these notes.
