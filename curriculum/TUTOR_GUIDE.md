# Tutor protocol and assessment rubric

This is an original teaching protocol, not an official HSK, TOPIK, or JLPT
marking scheme. Combine it with the selected level's syllabus and source notes.
Grammar entries and example translations are prompts for teaching, not substitutes
for checking meaning and natural usage.

For the independent Mandarin program, use
`chinese/teaching/program.yaml` for the numbered teaching levels and
`chinese/teaching/mastery.yaml` for per-item stages. Source HSK bands are
provenance, not a competence crosswalk. Keep that item's modality-specific
evidence separate from curriculum position, inventory coverage, and the
numeric analytic rubric below. Its stages are not numeric conversions of
these scores. The standalone tourist route shares sense IDs but has its own
practical goals and prerequisites.

Some level syllabi provide their own task-specific scales and thresholds. Use
those scales for those exit tasks, and this shared scale for cross-lesson
tracking. Record the rubric name and maximum with every score; never mix a
local 0-4 score or a 20-point total with this guide's 0-3 dimensions.

## A lesson packet

Retain the language, level, standard version, lesson goal, vocabulary IDs, grammar
IDs, prerequisite IDs or descriptions, and source references. Include a small
set of target/English pairs, practice prompts, acceptable answers, and a transfer
task. Keep assessment answers separate from the learner-visible prompt.

When using imported reading, preserve the original location of each occurrence.
Match phrases before individual words. Select the appropriate dictionary sense;
do not mechanically replace a word with every gloss in its vocabulary row.
If a needed term is outside the inventory, supply a translated support item and
mark it as supplemental, not as an official member of the selected level.

Use this teaching cycle:

1. **Diagnose:** Ask for a brief unaided comprehension or production task using
   prerequisites. Check writing-system knowledge independently from vocabulary.
2. **Model:** Present a target example and its English equivalent. Explain only
   the distinction needed now, including social context and register.
3. **Notice:** Ask the learner to identify the intended meaning, construction,
   and relevant word forms. Use a contrasting example with an English equivalent.
4. **Practice:** Move from recognition to reconstruction and free production.
   Fade hints; record whether an answer was independent.
5. **Transfer:** Use an unseen context, a different lexical combination, and a
   personally relevant task. Permit valid alternatives, not just an exact string.
6. **Review:** Revisit after an interval and in a different context. Shorten the
   interval for a misunderstood sense, construction, or repeatedly hinted item.

During assessment, English can be hidden from the learner. It must still be
stored in the tutor's packet for every target-language item, including complete
dialogue turns, example phrases in notes, corrected answers, and distractors.

## Shared analytic rubric

Score each relevant dimension separately from 0 to 3. Record "not assessed" for
skills the session cannot observe; never convert it to a passing score.

| Dimension | 0: not yet demonstrated | 1: supported | 2: independent | 3: transferable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning/comprehension | Main message is missed | Main message understood with hints; important details missed | Main message and relevant details understood without hints | Inference, intent, and contextual nuance handled in new material |
| Vocabulary | Required meaning cannot be expressed or recognized | Correct choice depends on translation/reveal; sense errors recur | Suitable words and senses used independently for the task | Appropriate paraphrase, collocation, and flexible sense selection |
| Grammar | Construction prevents the intended message | Pattern works only with model/hints or repeated correction | Target construction conveys intended meaning in a new sentence | Construction is varied and contrasted appropriately with alternatives |
| Task/discourse | Task outcome not achieved | Partial outcome; sequencing requires support | Task completed with level-appropriate coherence | Reasoning and organization adapt to a new audience or constraint |
| Register/pragmatics | Social choice substantially misrepresents intent | Politeness or stance needs prompting | Appropriate formality, stance, and conversational action | Nuance and shifts of relationship/audience handled deliberately |
| Script/orthography | Writing/reading blocks task | Frequent decoding or transcription support | Level-appropriate reading and written forms are functional | New combinations and contextual ambiguity are handled fluently |
| Listening, when observed | Main audio message missed | Repetition or transcript needed for core meaning | Relevant information understood without transcript | Inference and variation handled in unseen audio |
| Speaking, when observed | Speech not intelligible enough for task | Intelligibility or interaction needs repeated support | Speech is intelligible and interaction completes the task | Pronunciation, repair, and discourse adapt to novel interaction |

The language-level syllabus defines the difficulty of the task. A score of 3
on a beginner task does not establish advanced proficiency.

## Progress decisions

These are configurable teaching recommendations, not psychometrically validated
cutoffs or predictions of an examination result:

- **Practice further:** A relevant dimension is 0 or 1, or performance depends
  on revealing the translation/model.
- **Lesson acquired provisionally:** Relevant dimensions reach at least 2 in two
  different unaided tasks, including one unseen context. Reassess after a delay.
- **Ready to attempt the next level:** Complete the current level's exit tasks
  independently, show delayed retention of sampled prerequisites, and identify
  remaining weak domains explicitly. Do not require memorization of every
  dictionary sense.

For a practical check, sample 20-30 vocabulary items across the lesson blocks,
5-8 constructions in unseen contexts, and the syllabus's integrated exit tasks.
Include recognition and production, and reassess weak items separately. Sampling
helps choose the next lesson; it is not proof of exhaustive inventory mastery.

An exam-oriented lesson may emphasize receptive tasks for JLPT or the relevant
TOPIK/HSK components, but the broader teaching curriculum can also include
speaking and writing. Do not report an invented official exam score based on
these additional activities.

## Correction and reliability

Accept equivalent natural answers and explain meaningful differences instead
of treating the stored English as the only possible translation. Distinguish
an incorrect proposition, a grammar error, an odd collocation, a register mismatch,
and an orthographic slip. Give a short correction with a bilingual example,
then ask for another unaided attempt.

Treat pinyin, kana readings, dictionary glosses, and automated difficulty
assignments as source data that can contain errors. Check suspect items against
the cited sources or a competent instructor. Do not silently replace a source
gloss: retain the original and record any locally reviewed correction separately.

Do not claim to hear a spoken answer in a text-only channel. For pronunciation,
tone, listening, or fluency assessments, use actual audio and an appropriate
human or technical evaluator. If these are unavailable, keep that skill
"not assessed" and continue with observable tasks.
