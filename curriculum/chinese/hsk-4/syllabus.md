# Band 4: explanation, comparison and sustained discussion

**Basis:** GF0025-2021 educational framework; 972 new normalized headwords
(3,181 cumulative) and 25 original grammar selections, `zh-hsk4-gNNN`.
This is not an official exhaustive grammar list or the 2025/2026 exam syllabus.

## Prerequisites and outcomes

Demonstrate band-3 narratives, common complements and simple connected writing.
Check topic continuity, definite objects, contrastive negation and basic
conditionals before adding denser discourse organization.

The learner can explain choices at work or study, negotiate a service problem,
recognize factual versus hypothetical concession, distinguish beliefs from
facts and write a coherent practical recommendation.

## Ordered recurring lesson blocks

Use roughly 10–16 new entries per session with adaptive reduction after weak
recall. Search the current CSV by both topics and English glosses; include
prior-band prerequisites and preserve the full unseen-ID queue.

After thematic passes, route every remaining unseen ID to a recurring block,
including all `general-and-abstract` rows. A module is not one lesson, and
successful role-plays do not establish exposure to the entire 972-row list.

## Practice and review

Use contrasting accounts of the same event, assumption-versus-evidence sorting,
appointment negotiation, note-taking from reviewed audio, and paragraph
reordering. Have the learner paraphrase an argument before evaluating it.
Practice an authentic distinction between a factual concession and a hypothetical
one, plus reference-point effects of early/late expectation adverbs.

Model with English equivalents and progressively remove scaffolding during
retrieval; all created examples retain an accessible English gloss. Review at
one, three, seven, fourteen and thirty days and return to weak contrasts early.
Every fifth session asks for a new application with vocabulary from three
previous modules. Do not silently treat dictionary proper-name senses as the
primary daily-life learning target.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

1. Give a four-minute comparison of two workable plans with two advantages,
   one concession and a recommendation; answer unprepared questions.
2. Resolve a service misunderstanding in ten turns, clarifying one mistaken
   belief and negotiating a concrete outcome without a supplied script.
3. Read 400–600 characters of original explanatory prose, distinguish main
   claim from supporting details with at least 80% accuracy; write a
   250–350-character account with causal and contrastive links.

Score 0–4 for **task achievement/cohesion**, **comprehension/inference**,
**lexis and grammar**, **intelligibility/script/register**, and **interactive
independence/repair**. A 4 satisfies all task conditions accurately and
independently; 3 conveys the main reasoning with only minor errors and one
clarification; 2 loses a major relation or needs repeated support; 1 gives
fragments without a sustained account; 0 gives no relevant evidence.
Teaching exit: 16/20, no dimension below 2, on a different second task.

Check introduction records for every new ID; sample 120 new plus 30 prior IDs
across themes, aiming for 85% recognition and 75% contextual production. Audit
errors by sense, register and complement, not just correct spelling. These
locally chosen task sizes and rubric values are not official HSK scoring rules.
