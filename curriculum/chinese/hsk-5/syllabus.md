# Band 5: argument, perspective and independent learning

**Basis:** GF0025-2021 educational framework. The folder adds 1,059 normalized
headwords (4,240 cumulative), with 25 authored constructs `zh-hsk5-gNNN`.
No exhaustive official grammar mapping or 2025/2026 exam alignment is claimed.

## Prerequisites and outcomes

Demonstrate band-4 explanations, connected paragraphs and flexible event
descriptions. Check that the learner can separate a cause, an intended purpose
and a condition; review relative clauses before formal nominal packaging.

The learner can justify priorities, understand qualified claims, summarize
perspectives, respond tactfully to disagreement and produce a structured
argument for a non-specialist audience.

## Ordered recurring lesson blocks

Introduce 12–18 new entries per session only if cumulative review is sustainable.
Combine topic search with English-gloss search, and record a practiced sense
for polysemous words. Bring forward prerequisites from bands 1–4.

Cycle through the themes, then allocate every remaining unseen ID—including
general/abstract and specialized entries—to another pass. The five modules
organize learning; they do not compress all 1,059 entries into five lessons.

## Practice and review

Use a short bilingual position statement, identify claim/evidence/assumption,
compare two paraphrases and explain which changes the author's certainty.
Translate a short practical message both ways, prioritizing intent and register
over word-for-word substitution. Rewrite a confrontational question as a polite
request. Teach rhetorical questions as pragmatic choices, not neutral queries.

Space retrieval over one, three, seven, fourteen and thirty days, then monthly
for fragile academic vocabulary. Every fifth session combines new and old
themes in a short debate. Keep separate comprehension and production records;
memorizing an English gloss is not proof of appropriate collocation.
All tutor-supplied target text must have a checked English equivalent.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

1. Present a five-minute recommendation with two supporting reasons, a plausible
   counterargument and a qualified response; answer at least three unscripted
   questions without changing the original claim accidentally.
2. Read two original 500–700-character texts with contrasting perspectives;
   identify each author's conclusion, evidence and one limitation.
3. Write a 400–500-character argument for a specified audience, then give a
   short English summary preserving its qualification. Translate a brief
   practical notice into Chinese without adding or deleting essential facts.

Score 0–4 in **task/argument structure**, **comprehension and source fidelity**,
**lexical/grammatical precision**, **register and intelligibility**, **independent
interaction/revision**. A 4 is accurate, organized and independent throughout;
3 meets the main conditions with a minor omission or recoverable slip; 2 has
one major unsupported inference, meaning-changing error or repeated prompting;
1 has fragmented reasoning or heavy dependence on a model; 0 gives no relevant
evidence. Teaching exit: 16/20, no dimension below 2, repeated with new material.

Audit all 1,059 introduction records. Use a stratified 150-ID current/prior
sample: aim for 90% recognition and 75% context-sensitive production, with
separate checks for register and implication. Task lengths and numerical
thresholds are authored local criteria, not official HSK marks or a prediction
of success on any particular examination.
