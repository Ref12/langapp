# Band 6: nuanced discourse and critical synthesis

**Basis:** GF0025-2021 educational framework; 1,123 incremental normalized
headwords (5,363 cumulative). The 25 patterns `zh-hsk6-gNNN` are original
teaching selections, not exhaustive official grammar or the 2025/2026 exam list.

## Prerequisites and outcomes

Demonstrate band-5 argumentation, uncertainty marking and audience-sensitive
writing. Diagnose counterfactuals, double negation, source attribution and
conditional inference before increasing information density.

The learner can synthesize contrasting reports, distinguish speculation from
evidence, qualify a judgment, conduct a substantive interview and revise a
longer argument for clarity, coherence and appropriate register.

## Ordered recurring lesson blocks

Use about 12–18 new entries per session when retrieval remains reliable.
Select by English definitions and vocabulary topics, then check actual sense and
collocation. Recycle lower-band grammar to avoid formal-language overuse.

Continue thematic cycles and audit the unintroduced tail. Every new row,
including the general/abstract remainder, must eventually receive a module
assignment. Progress in a discussion does not establish inventory completion.

## Practice and review

Annotate an original bilingual passage for evidence, claim, qualification and
rhetorical emphasis. Compare neutral and evaluative adverbs. Reconstruct an
argument from notes, then change its audience from a colleague to the public.
Use verified audio for listening summaries and actual audio for pronunciation
feedback; never infer speech competence from a typed answer.

Apply one-, three-, seven-, fourteen- and thirty-day retrieval with monthly
maintenance. Add contrastive mini-reviews for formal versus conversational
synonyms and logical scope. Every fifth session uses a new cross-theme
problem. A revised answer must fix the original meaning error, not merely use
more advanced vocabulary. Tutor-created passages and corrections always retain
English equivalents; do not fabricate sources for reported claims.

## Exit tasks and observable rubric

1. Give a six-minute evidence-based briefing from two supplied original texts,
   preserving each source's certainty and clearly identifying disagreement.
   Handle four unprepared challenges with at most one clarification.
2. Read 800–1,000 characters of analytical prose and identify thesis, two lines
   of support, a concession and a limitation with at least 85% accuracy.
3. Write a 600–800-character recommendation, revise one paragraph for a
   different audience, and translate a short summary both ways without
   adding facts or reversing a condition.

Score **organization/task achievement**, **comprehension and attribution**,
**lexical/grammatical/logical precision**, **register/intelligibility**, and
**interaction/self-editing** from 0–4. A 4 is complete, independent and precise;
3 satisfies the main conditions with minor nonessential slips; 2 includes a
major distortion or repeated need for support; 1 depends heavily on copied
frames or disconnected claims; 0 supplies no relevant evidence. Suggested exit:
17/20 with every dimension at least 3 on two unfamiliar topics.

Verify all 1,123 rows have been introduced and test 160 new plus 40 previous
IDs, targeting 90% recognition and 80% context-sensitive production. Report
uncertain dictionary senses separately. These are local instructional
requirements, not an official score conversion or proof of exhaustive grammar
mastery. Current examination task practice remains a separate, version-checked
activity using authorized materials.
